# Competitor research: Controlled Insanity, GASP, Breathe Divinity

**Purpose:** find where these three brands are strong, then make SWOLVATION distinct from them. Nothing here is to be copied. No ads, slogans, logos, layouts or designs from these brands were used or recreated.

**Method (11 Oct 2026):**
- Fetched each brand's own storefront at phone width and screenshotted it.
- Read each site's meta descriptions.
- Read public third-party summaries: a Loox review page, a Motion ad-library summary for GASP, and trademark records.

**Limits:**
- I couldn't open the Meta Ad Library or TikTok Creative Center from this environment. Ad-level findings below therefore come from the storefronts and third-party summaries, not from a full audit of their paid ads.
- Before you spend, look each brand up in the Meta Ad Library (facebook.com/ads/library). It's free and shows their live ads.

## What each brand does

### Controlled Insanity (controlledinsanity.com)
- **Product:** oversized "pump cover" tees, waffle knit, crewnecks, hoodies, sweats, jerseys. The heavy oversized fit is what customers praise most, per about 3,900 Loox reviews.
- **Storefront:**
  - The hero is a hooded model shot from behind, with big heavy sans-serif type ("WAFFLE KNIT") and a "NOW LIVE / SHOP NOW" block.
  - A "LIMITED TIME 10% OFF" email pop-up appears immediately.
- **Strengths:**
  - A clear product hero.
  - A big community of real customers, whose reviews appear on site.
  - An obvious buy path.
- **Weakness we can exploit:** the look is the generic modern gymwear template (model, big sans headline, discount pop-up). Nothing about it is ownable.

### GASP (gaspofficial.com)
- **Positioning:** "bodybuilding clothing", old-school hardcore heritage, "Throwback Era" and "Old School | New School" lines. Its stated values are No Compromises, Being Relentless and No Excuses.
- **Storefront:** a full-screen cookie wall first, then product grids.
- **Ads:** a third-party (Motion) summary describes a mix of polished shots and raw, UGC-style footage built around community and training travel.
- **Strengths:**
  - Deep authenticity in bodybuilding culture.
  - Heritage.
  - Rugged product language (heavyweight cotton, built for bodybuilders).
- **Weakness we can exploit:** the ads are people-driven and athlete-led, and the language is loud. They rely on physiques. We can't, and won't.

### Breathe Divinity (breathedivinity.com)

The brief lists breathedivinity.org. Fetching that domain timed out from this environment, so the research below uses breathedivinity.com, the brand's storefront, which describes itself as Breathedivinity.
- **Positioning:** "a luxury gym brand, featuring unique intricate designs, cut-and-sew garments, exclusive drops." Despite the name, it is not positioned as a Christian brand: its language is mystic and alternative, not scripture.
- **Storefront:**
  - Password-gated drop pages with a live countdown.
  - Gothic serif collection names in red and black.
  - Grunge texture.
  - A large "DROPS ALWAYS SELL OUT. BE FIRST." SMS-capture overlay.
- **Strengths:**
  - The strongest art direction of the three.
  - Drop culture creates urgency and an event.
  - Intricate graphics as the hero.
- **Weakness we can exploit:**
  - The urgency is manufactured: countdowns and "always sell out".
  - It is model-led.
  - The gothic look has no meaning underneath it.

## Patterns across all three

| Pattern | Controlled Insanity | GASP | Breathe Divinity | SWOLVATION response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A human body is the hero | Yes | Yes | Yes | **The garment is the hero.** No people, by rule |
| Urgency mechanics (discounts, countdowns, "sell out") | 10% pop-up | — | Countdown, "always sell out" | **No invented urgency.** "Drop 001" names the release; it doesn't threaten |
| Loud copy | Medium | High ("No excuses") | Medium | **Quiet copy.** Few words, scripture does the talking |
| Dark, gothic graphic language | Some | Some (camo, rugged) | Strong | Shared territory, but ours has **meaning**: every skull and cross sits under a real verse |
| Community proof | Thousands of reviews | Athletes, travel | Waitlists | We have none yet. **Don't fake it.** Earn it after launch |

## Where SWOLVATION is ownable

1. **Faith and iron, sincerely.** None of the three puts scripture on the garment and means it. Joshua 1:9 and Philippians 4:13 are the product, not decoration.
2. **Silence as a brand device.** Everyone else is loud. An ad that is mostly room tone, iron and one line of type stands out in a Reels feed.
3. **The relic treatment.** Shirts shot like objects in a sacred, worn place: hard single light, chalk dust, concrete. Product-first, no bodies. The ad holds still long enough to read the print.
4. **Honesty as positioning.** No countdowns, no fake reviews, no invented fabric claims. With a new brand, credibility is the asset.

## Implications for the ads

- Lead with the **back print**. It is the thing nobody else has.
- Keep **text short and set in the brand fonts** (Big Shoulders and Archivo), centred inside platform safe zones.
- **Sound design over music.** Room tone, iron, one impact. This also avoids music licensing risk entirely.
- **Conversion ads stay plain:**
  - Show back, detail, front and full piece.
  - Name the product.
  - Give one CTA.
- Don't imitate drop-culture urgency. If a real limited run or date ever exists, say so plainly. Until then, "Drop 001" only labels this as the first release.
